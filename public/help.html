2. Quick start

  1. Click 💡 Load Example to populate an eGFP sequence and five test mutations.
  2. Click ▶ Design Primers.
  3. Inspect the Cards view — mutated bases are highlighted in amber.
  4. Switch to Order List for the deduplicated set of oligos.
  5. Click ⬇ Export Order List to download a two-column CSV ready to paste into a supplier order form.

Then replace the example with your own sequence and mutations.

3. Inputs

3.1 DNA sequence

Paste the coding sequence of your gene, from the A of the start codon, in frame. Raw sequence or FASTA both work; FASTA header lines beginning with > are stripped automatically, whether you paste them or load them from file. All non-ACGT characters (whitespace, line numbers, ambiguity codes) are discarded before translation.

📂 Load FASTA accepts .fasta, .fa, and .txt. The nucleotide count appears next to the buttons once a sequence is loaded — check it is a multiple of 3 and matches your expected ORF length.

⚠️ Paste the gene, not the whole plasmid. Numbering and translation both assume the sequence starts at codon 1 (or at a fixed offset — see Frame shift).

3.2 Organism (codon usage)

Selects the codon assigned to each substituted amino acid.

Option Table Source
E. coli E. coli K-12 most frequent codons Hershberg & Petrov 2008
S. cer. S. cerevisiae most frequent codons Sharp & Li 1987
P. pas. P. pastoris (K. phaffii) most frequent codons Xu et al. 2021

One preferred codon per amino acid is used; the tool does not sample alternative synonymous codons.

3.3 Mutation list

Standard one-letter protein nomenclature: [WT residue][position][new residue], e.g. D110A. Separate entries with commas, spaces, or newlines — all three are accepted interchangeably.

The WT residue you type is verified against the translated sequence. A mismatch raises an error rather than designing a wrong primer, so it doubles as a frame check.

Degenerate codons for saturation mutagenesis use two reserved letters in the "new residue" position:

Code Codon Coverage
X NNK All 20 amino acids, 32 codons, one stop (TAG)
Z NDT 12 amino acids, 12 codons, no stops, no redundancy

So G21X orders an NNK library at position 21; G21Z orders the balanced NDT subset. Both can be listed alongside defined mutants at the same site — see Shared primers.

3.4 Mutation mode

Determines which primer carries the mutation. This is the single most consequential choice for cost.

Mode Behaviour Use when
⇄ Both Both primers carry the mutant codon; symmetric overlap design Single defined mutants; most robust amplification
→ Fwd only FWD carries the mutation; REV is pure wild-type Several variants at one site — the WT reverse primer is shared
← Rev only REV carries the mutation; FWD is pure wild-type As above, mirrored; useful if the downstream flank has poor Tm or a repeat

In fwd/rev mode the wild-type primer is identical for every variant at that position, so a five-variant site needs six oligos instead of ten.

3.5 Parameters

Parameter Default What it controls
Frame shift 0 Nucleotide offset added to the residue number, for constructs whose numbering does not start at codon 1 — e.g. -9 when the sequence includes a 3-residue N-terminal extension not counted in the published numbering
Target Tm (°C) 60 Tm target for the priming region of the mutation-carrying primer
Min anchor (nt) 17 Minimum priming region length, enforced before Tm is checked
Tm priming (°C) Tm − 10 Tm target for the pure wild-type shared primer in fwd/rev mode. Leave blank to use the default

Priming regions grow one base at a time from the mutation site until both the minimum anchor length and the Tm target are satisfied, so raising Min anchor and raising Target Tm both lengthen primers — and cost.

4. Reading the results

4.1 Summary bar

Box Meaning
PROCESSED Mutations parsed, including failed ones
WARNINGS Designs that succeeded but tripped a quality check
ERRORS Mutations that could not be designed
TOTAL COST Sum of unique primers only, at SGD 0.18/bp

4.2 Case convention

Within every primer sequence:

  • UPPERCASE = bases changed relative to wild type (highlighted amber)
  • lowercase = wild-type bases

For degenerate codons all three bases are uppercase, since every position is variable.

4.3 Card fields

Each card reports, per primer: length, Tm, GC%, and the codon change in the form D(gat)→(gcg)A. The mutated full-length DNA sequence is also stored and exported — use it as the reference for sequencing verification.

4.4 Warnings

Warning Threshold Suggested response
Fwd/Rev self-dimer Any 5-nt self-complementary stretch Usually tolerable; if the PCR fails, shift the anchor by changing Min anchor by ±2
Fwd/Rev GC x% Outside 40–65% Raise Target Tm to extend into more balanced flanking sequence
Fwd/Rev Tm low Below 55 °C Raise Target Tm or Min anchor; check the flank is not AT-rich or repetitive

Warnings are advisory. A flagged primer is still exported and will often work — treat them as a ranked to-do list if amplification fails.

4.5 Shared primers

When a primer sequence has already appeared earlier in the run, it is tagged ♻ Reused from mutation and priced at SGD 0. This is what makes fwd/rev mode economical for site-saturation panels: G21A, G21X, and G21Z in fwd mode all share one reverse primer.

The Order List tab shows only the primers you actually need to buy, named <mutation>_Fwd / <mutation>_Rev; reused ones are excluded.

4.6 Exports

Button File Contents
⬇ Export CSV primer_design_results.csv Full record: both primers, lengths, Tm, GC%, codon change, per-primer cost, warnings, shared-primer notes, mutated DNA sequence, and a total row
⬇ Export Order List primer_order_list.csv Two columns — primer name and sequence — deduplicated, for direct submission to an oligo supplier

Keep the full CSV with your construct records; it documents exactly what was ordered and why.

5. Error messages

Message Cause Fix
Cannot parse position from "..." Malformed entry, e.g. Ala110Ala or D-110A Use one-letter format: D110A
Position N out of range (protein length L) Residue number beyond the ORF, or a frame problem Check the sequence is the full CDS and that Frame shift is correct
Expected D at position 110, found G The WT residue does not match the translation at that position Almost always a numbering offset — set Frame shift. Otherwise confirm you pasted the right gene or the right frame
Unknown amino acid "B" for this organism Letter is not a standard residue, *, X, or Z Use a valid one-letter code

The Expected ... found ... error is the most useful diagnostic in the tool: if it fires on every mutation, your numbering is offset by a constant, and Frame shift will fix all of them at once.

6. How primers are calculated

Melting temperature. Nearest-neighbour thermodynamics (SantaLucia 1998) with the Owczarzy et al. 2004 salt correction, evaluated at 50 mM Na⁺ and 250 nM primer. Values are calibrated to agree with Biopython Tm_NN(saltcorr=7).

Priming region growth. Starting from the mutant codon, the region is extended base by base — leftward for the reverse primer, rightward for the forward primer — until both the minimum anchor length and the Tm target are met. For degenerate codons the wild-type sequence is used for the Tm calculation, since nearest-neighbour parameters are undefined for ambiguous bases.

Primer architecture. In all three modes the primer pair shares a 12-nt overlap spanning the mutation site. The amplicon therefore carries a 12-bp terminal direct repeat.

GC content is computed over the whole primer, including the overlap region.

7. Downstream protocol

Adapt this section to your lab's standard workflow.

The 12-bp terminal overlap means the linear PCR product is recircularised by overlap-dependent assembly — HiFi/Gibson assembly, In-Fusion, or in vivo recombination in a recombination-competent E. coli strain — rather than by blunt-end ligation, which would duplicate the overlap.

A typical run:

  1. High-fidelity PCR across the whole plasmid using the designed pair. Extension time scaled to the full plasmid length.
  2. DpnI digestion of the template (37 °C, 1 h).
  3. Recircularisation by the assembly method above.
  4. Transformation and colony PCR.
  5. Sanger sequencing across the mutation site, verified against the Mutated DNA Sequence column of the exported CSV.

For NNK/NDT libraries, pool sufficient transformants for the target coverage before sequencing a representative sample to confirm codon distribution.

8. Limitations

  • One preferred codon per amino acid; no synonymous-codon optimisation or restriction-site avoidance.
  • Self-dimer detection is a simple 5-nt complementarity scan, not a full thermodynamic secondary-structure prediction.
  • No hairpin, primer-dimer (cross-pair), or template mispriming check — verify against the full plasmid if the target has paralogous or repeated regions.
  • Insertions, deletions, and multi-residue changes in a single primer are not supported; only single-codon substitutions.
  • Cost is fixed at SGD 0.18/bp and reflects one supplier's standard desalted synthesis. Treat it as a relative ranking, not a quotation.
